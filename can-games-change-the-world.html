Can Games Change the World?

An 8-week speculative game design class at boshi's place

Overview

Sometime around the late 2000s, certain people proclaimed that games “would change the world”. And how could they not? They bring people together, there were studies coming out left-and-right declaring the educational and pro-social benefits of games, and more people were gaming than ever before. Indeed, it felt like we were entering a “ludic century”.

But it didn’t happen. The world didn’t change (...or at least not in a necessarily positive way). So what went wrong? Is there still a chance that games could change the world? And if not, what else should we do with our art?

In this class, we will critically play and examine various types of games that were made (or been said) to “change the world” (whatever that may mean in their certain contexts): educational games, political & documentary games, empathy games, gamification, etc. We will read and discuss studies and articles promoting or criticizing these games, as well as draw from art theory & philosophy on the “point” of art and other art practices that try to create social change. We will imagine, speculate, and craft games that explore alternate ways to create “change”. By the end of class, students will have a grasp on many kinds of ways people have tried to change the world with games, made some speculative games, and formulated their own individual theory on whether games can change the world.

Is this class for me?

This class is for you if you:
  • are an artist or game developer interested in figuring out how to make a meaningful change in the world as part of their practice.
  • are disillusioned with the status quo.
  • ever wondered while watching The Game Awards what the hell is a “game for impact”.
  • want to imagine alternative & speculative ways to make games. want to meet in a physical or online space to discuss art & games with others.
  • like playing and reading about games and why we make art.
  • feel any sort of way about educational games, gamification, or games for impact.
  • had your mind changed by a piece of art.
  • never had your mind changed by a piece of art.
  • don’t necessarily consider yourself a gamer but want to learn more about a specific kind of games.
  • want to change the world.
Of course, the question of “can games change the world” is impossible to answer (what do we count as the world? and what do we define as change? how can we even measure it?). The point of the class is not to come to a rigid yes/no, but rather to get a sense of everything that’s already been tried, not repeat the same mistakes, and think deeper about what we can do to make the world a better place and how games fit within that goal. °l||l° °l||l° °l||l° °l||l° °l||l° °l||l° °l||l°

What is a speculative game?

Basically, it’s an imagined game for imagined situations that don’t exist (or haven't happened yet). Here’s an example of some speculative games I’ve come up. The idea is inspired by speculative design, which is a set of related practices that "remove the constraints from the commercial sector that define normative design processes [...] and use fiction to present alternative products, systems or worlds” (Auger). The point is to “ask questions rather than provide answers” and “encourage people to question [...] how things could be different” (Dunne & Raby). As activities in a classroom, the point is to imagine beyond the unseen confines of our current practice. For some examples, you can look into the work of Ilya & Emilia Kabakov, Fluxus scores, or Ursula Le Guin. °l||l° °l||l° °l||l° °l||l° °l||l° °l||l° °l||l°
